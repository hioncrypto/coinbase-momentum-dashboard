Wah rocking motion

Heel = darker / more closed filter. Toe = brighter / more open. The intro often starts parked about ⅔ toward toe (nasal), then rocks with each lick.

HEELclosed · dark TOEopen · bright

OPEN ~35% · toward heel

1 · ParkStart ~⅔ open for the nasal intro growl.
2 · Rock outAs the lick rises, push toe (brighter).
3 · Rock backAs the lick falls / chord hits, heel (darker).
4 · Chord stabsOften more open on E7♯9 hits — “wah!” accent.

Muted string chugging + pick motion

Correction from the SRV · Live from Austin performance: the scratchy intro mute is fretting-hand muting on the neck (fingers resting on the strings without fretting), not a metal-style palm mute at the bridge. The picking hand strums through those deadened strings while the wah talks.

Muting hand · on the NECK (what the video shows)

In the Austin clip, the fretting hand lays across the strings around the mid-neck (~7th fret). Fingers touch the strings but do not press them to the frets — that kills pitch and makes the wah “chk-chk” scrapes (xxxxxx).

Picking hand · strums; not the main mute

The picking hand mainly strums / picks through the already-deadened strings. A light bridge touch can happen in blues, but for this intro’s signature scrapes the mute you see in the video is on the fretboard.

What that Austin video shows (plain language)

1. Fretting fingers rest on top of the strings on the neck (often near ~7th fret for SRV’s muted chatter).
2. He is not squeezing the strings down to the wood — no clear fretted pitch, only a dead scrape.
3. Picking hand keeps a rhythmic ↓↑ / strum pulse while the wah rocks — the “voice” is filter + dead strings.
4. When the real notes start (hammer-ons around frets 5–9, then the low-E riff), the fretting hand presses normally and the mute lifts off those scrapes.
My earlier “palm mute at the bridge” diagram was the wrong primary location for this part.

How to mute (matching the video)
1. Lay fretting fingers across the strings around ~fret 7.
2. Touch only — do not push strings to the fretboard.
3. Strum / pick ↓↑ through those dead strings + rock the wah.
4. When real notes start, press normally; lift the “resting mute.”
5. Bridge palm mute is optional extras — it is NOT the main mute you see here.
Pick motion
↓ Down on accents through the muted strings.
↑ Up on the off-beats — keeps the scratch groove alive.
